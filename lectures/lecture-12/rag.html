RAG as a Systems Problem

How can a language model answer questions about your documents? Retrieve relevant passages, supply them as context, and measure whether the answers are supported.
Daniel G. Graham · UVA School of Data Science

A language model cannot reliably answer questions about documents it has not been given. Its parametric knowledge comes from training, and its context window limits how much text you can send in one request. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) addresses this by finding relevant passages and adding them to the prompt. This lecture focuses on the database work behind retrieval: storing chunks and metadata, indexing embeddings, ranking results, and evaluating them. Thursday’s lab combines those pieces into a pipeline you can use as a starting point for the project.1

Companion reading: Lewis et al., Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks (the paper that named it)1 · Liu et al., Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts · pgvector + Postgres full-text: hybrid search in one query2.

Retrieval turns the problem into a database workload

Retrieval lets you update source documents and their index without retraining the model. Each question sends only selected passages to the generator. Retaining source identifiers also gives the answer a citation trail that can be checked.

Missing evidence limits the answer



Retrieval determines which source passages the model can use. If a passage is missing from the prompt, a better prompt cannot make the model quote it. Generation still needs evaluation: a model can misread, ignore, or misrepresent passages that were retrieved correctly.



Describe the pipeline with four related tables



The project proposal asks for four tables with keys: documents, chunks, embeddings, and eval_questions. The foreign key from chunks.doc_id to documents.doc_id connects a retrieved passage to its source. The other relationships connect vectors and evaluation labels to those records. Overlap and metadata are record design too




  
OverlapA small overlap, such as 10–15%, can preserve claims that cross chunk boundaries. Measure the effect: overlap also duplicates text and can return several near-identical chunks.6

  
MetadataStore the document id, title, and position with each chunk. The lab’s doc and title fields let the assembled context identify its source.

  
Record designChunking decides what one retrievable record contains. A text splitter implements those boundaries, but you still need to choose boundaries and metadata that preserve the meaning of the source.7

Retrieval quality is measurable: two numbers, twelve questions



Two numbers from one hand-labeled set





The eval set is 12 pairs of (question, relevant doc ids), labeled by hand, once. Two numbers
come out of it: hit@k and MRR, the mean reciprocal rank8:

\[ \text{hit@}k \;=\; \frac{\#\{\text{questions with a relevant doc in the top } k\}}{\#\text{questions}} \qquad \text{(did it come back?)} \]
\[ \text{MRR} \;=\; \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{q \in Q} \frac{1}{\text{rank}_q} \qquad \text{(how high?)} \]

where rankq is the position of the first relevant doc: rank 1 scores 1.0, rank 2 scores
0.5, rank 3 scores 0.33, and a doc that never appears scores 0.





Embedding width changed hit@3 from 0.42 to 0.92



Thursday’s hashed bag-of-words embedder maps each word to one of DIM vector positions and increments that position. With fewer positions, unrelated words collide more often, making unrelated texts appear similar. The evaluation set measures how these collisions affect retrieval:9

A small evaluation set makes configurations comparable



Changing DIM improved hit@3 from 0.42 to 0.92 on these 12 questions. That is evidence about this corpus and question set, not a guarantee for new questions. Your project needs its own labeled set so you can compare configurations and inspect the remaining failures.

Vocabulary mismatchTwo questions can ask for the same information while using different words. A bag-of-words embedder cannot reliably recognize that relationship.

  
Why it happensThe buffer-pool document shares few content words with “popular pages in memory.” Its low score reflects limited word overlap rather than whether it answers the question.

  
The fixA learned embedding model can place paraphrases near one another. In the lab design, you replace the embed() function and rebuild the vectors. Evaluate the new model to check which failures it fixes.





A retrieved passage may still be ranked or used poorly




  
Lost in the middleLiu et al. found that the tested models used relevant evidence less reliably when it appeared in the middle of a long context. Try fewer chunks or a different order, then evaluate whether the change helps.1011

  
Right doc, wrong rankA relevant chunk at rank 3 counts as a hit@3, just as one at rank 1 does. MRR distinguishes them: rank 3 contributes 0.33, while rank 1 contributes 1.0. A reranker can change a poor ranking by scoring the question and chunk together.





Keep the index current and check answer support




  
Stale indexWhen a document changes but its chunks or vectors do not, retrieval can return outdated information. Rebuild the affected records on update and record document and index versions so stale entries can be detected.

  
Confident grounding failureA model may give a confident answer even when the retrieved chunks do not support it. Evaluate unsupported-answer cases, calibrate any score thresholds on your data, and allow the generator to say that the sources do not answer the question.

  
Embedding driftQuery and document vectors must use a compatible embedding model. When changing models, record the model version, rebuild the corpus vectors, and switch queries to the new index together. Do not compare vectors from incompatible embedding spaces.





Fabricated citations have already reached a courtroom




  Case study · Mata v. Avianca, 2023
  
In Mata v. Avianca (2023), lawyers submitted nonexistent opinions generated by ChatGPT and were sanctioned. The case illustrates why plausible text is not evidence. Retrieval can supply real source passages, but the system must still check that each citation exists and supports the claim. Carry source identifiers through the pipeline and inspect citation failures in your evaluation. Source: the court’s order.






Improve retrieval with additional search stages



The production retrieval stack



Three common additions can improve the lab pipeline. Each adds work, so use evaluation results to decide whether it is useful for your corpus:




  
UpgradeWhat it isDatabase idea underneath

  
    
Hybrid searchrun BM25 keyword search AND vector search, merge the ranked lists13combine two retrieval methods over the same corpus. Keyword search can recover exact identifiers such as error codes; vector search can recover paraphrases. Merge their ranked results and evaluate whether the combined list is better

    
Rerankingretrieve a generous top-50 cheaply, then let a reranker model re-order and keep 514use an inexpensive first stage to select candidates, then apply a more expensive model to that smaller set. The reduction in candidates makes reranking practical

    
Metadata filteringrestrict the similarity search to chunks whose stored metadata matches a predicate, for example "top-k most similar AND team = 'infra' AND date > 2025"combine a metadata predicate with nearest-neighbor search. Check whether the engine filters before, during, or after approximate search: the choice affects both result count and work15

FAQ



Context windows keep growing: will long context kill RAG?


A longer context window lets you send more text, but does not guarantee that the model will use every passage effectively. Cost, latency, and evidence selection still matter. Compare full-context and retrieval approaches on your own corpus when both are practical.11



Why not fine-tune on our documents instead?


Fine-tuning can adapt behavior, but it does not provide a dependable lookup of specific passages or a source citation trail. Retrieval is useful when answers must reflect changing documents. The two approaches can be combined: adapt model behavior and retrieve current evidence.3



Is the hashed bag-of-words embedder just a toy?


It uses the same feature-hashing idea as scikit-learn’s HashingVectorizer. Its vocabulary limitation is intentional: the lab makes that failure visible and measurable. Replacing embed() with a learned model keeps the pipeline interfaces, but requires rebuilding the index and rerunning the evaluation.9



What should our project's eval set look like?


Write at least 25 questions a real user of your corpus would ask, each labeled with the source passages that answer it. Compare at least two configurations and report retrieval and answer quality. Keep the partner team’s questions held out while tuning, then report those results alongside your own. The proposal outlines this plan; the final report supplies the evidence.
